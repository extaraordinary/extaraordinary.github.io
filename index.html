About

I am a doctoral candidate in Materials Science and Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Dr. Ismaila Dabo. (You can watch a short overview of our research group's vision for AI and sustainable energy here). My research develops predictive models for how atomic-scale disorder in complex oxides governs heat and vibrational transport, work aimed at accelerating materials design and discovery for next-generation energy conversion and thermal management systems.

My core doctoral work centers on predictive modeling of phonons in high-entropy oxides for thermal energy harvesting. To probe these expansive compositional spaces, I combine high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) with applied machine learning techniques such as graph neural network (GNN) interatomic potentials, running automated, high-performance computing (HPC) workflows that make tractable calculations that would otherwise be computationally prohibitive.

An overarching theme in my work is the tradeoff between physical interpretability and computational efficiency. This raises several open questions I actively pursue: How can vibrational modes be meaningfully defined and tracked in strongly disordered materials? What structural information is lost when complexity is reduced to parent components, and what new atomistic descriptors are needed to capture emergent behavior? How can data-driven models be designed to strictly respect underlying physical constraints?

To address these questions, my research draws on specialized methods including phonon band unfolding, non-analytical corrections to dynamical matrices, and the virtual crystal approximation, built on automated DFT workflow pipelines that scale materials screening across large HPC clusters. My work is inherently collaborative; I engage with theorists and experimentalists as a member of the Center for Nanoscale Science, a Materials Research Science and Engineering Center (MRSEC) supported by the National Science Foundation, along with other multi-institutional networks.

I'm always happy to discuss research, cool ideas, or potential collaborations — feel free to reach out, and I welcome any conversations that challenge me. Definitely feel free to reach out! :-)

Fun

Playing the piano has long been a central part of my life. I enjoy exploring a wide range of music that spans from the Romantic era—particularly the works of Chopin and Liszt—to contemporary composers such as Hans Zimmer, Max Richter, Ludovico Einaudi, and Yann Tiersen. Occasionally, I compose minimalist pieces of my own or reinterpret modern songs that resonate with me. You can listen to a performance of Claude Debussy's Piano Trio in G Major by our trio chamber ensemble, as well as our piano quintet's interpretation of Queen's Bohemian Rhapsody.

Traveling and learning about new cultures constantly inspires me. I find delight in wandering around new places, discovering local art, savoring regional cuisine, and capturing the quiet rythms of daily life through photography. I also enjoy hiking, camping, and skiing; any excuse to be outdoors and feel connected to nature.

I have recently discovered a deep appreciation for woodworking, and find it truly grounding and rewarding. Learning about CMU's TechSpark makerspace was a natural entry point, and I had the opportunity of benefiting from the tools and community to turn ideas into some cool, tangible objects.
